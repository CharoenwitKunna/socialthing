Tackling Thailand’s Inequality & High Cost of Living

The richest 10% of people in Thailand own more than half of the country's wealth. At the same time, poorer families spend almost half of all their money just to buy food. We can solve this by bringing communities together to demand new laws from the government.

43.3% Income gap score in 2021 (highest in East Asia & Pacific)
>50% Of total wealth owned by richest 10%
48.5% Of income spent on food by poorer families

The Problem & Conflict Theory

Big Gap

In 2021, Thailand had the highest income gap in East Asia and the Pacific. The richest 10% owned more than half of the country's entire wealth.

Families Struggling

Lower-income workers, informal laborers, and families with young children suffer the most from expensive food and high everyday living costs.

Children from lower income groups have less opportunities

When families spend most of their income on food and other basic needs, they have less money for education. As a result, children from poorer families may have fewer educational opportunities, achieve lower test scores, leave school early, and be less likely to attend university.

Conflict Theory

We chose Conflict Theory to explain Thailand's situation. This sociological framework shows how society divides between those who own major economic capital and working families who do not. Because wealth and institutional influence concentrate heavily at the top, market rules naturally favor owners over laborers. Struggling families must spend almost all their earnings on daily survival, leaving them with minimal leverage in the broader economy.

Source: World Bank: Bridging the Gap in Thailand

The Main Cause & How Society Should Look

The Main Cause: Limited Food leading to Higher Prices

When farming production drops, there is less food in the market. With less food available, prices go up. In Thailand, food is what families spend most of their income on. When food gets more expensive, families who are already having a hard time fall even deeper into poverty.

Source: UNICEF: Rising Living Costs in Thailand

How Society Should Look

Equality

Fair for Everyone

Society should be equal and welcoming, where everyone can access basic needs and everyday services without falling behind.

Safety Net

Help for Families

The government should give direct, dependable help to low-income families that are struggling with rising expenses.

Public Investment

Better Public Services

Public funding must support quality schools, hospitals, and care facilities so everyday living costs don't overwhelm ordinary people.

Source: UNICEF: Social Policy in Thailand

How to Make Social Change

1. Pushing for Better Laws

This mechanism works because the government can change laws that directly control workers' pay. Thailand's wage committee already looks at living costs when deciding minimum wages. Pushing for better laws means asking the government to raise minimum wages fairly so workers can pay for food and basic needs.

2. Community Organizing

Laws only pass and work when everyday people stand together. By bringing workers, farmers, and neighborhoods together, communities gain real power to make leaders listen and pass rules that help ordinary people instead of only big companies.

Source: Ministry of Labour: Minimum Wage Updates

Action Plan & Backup Plan

Quick Help for Regular People

Changing big monopoly rules takes a very long time. Instead, our main strategy uses two quick actions:

Step 1 Direct Relief

Send Money Directly

Send cash support straight to struggling families so they can buy food right away.

Step 2 Price Control

Set Price Caps

Put a price ceiling on key foods (like rice, oil, eggs, and pork). A price ceiling means companies are not allowed to charge higher than a set price.

Possible Obstacle & Solution: If companies hide food or refuse to sell because of price caps, the government can check their warehouses and allow food from other countries to enter with no extra fees to keep food on shelves.

Backup Plan: Wealth Distribution

If price caps do not work, the government can use wealth distribution:

Action A Progressive Tax

Collect More from the Rich

The government takes more tax money from the wealthiest people and biggest corporations who have extra riches.

Action B Social Programs

Give to People Who Need It

Use that money to directly help lower-income families with free healthcare, school food, and education support.

Source: Study.com: Government Wealth Distribution
